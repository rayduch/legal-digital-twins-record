## Question(s) presented (verbatim from the Court's QP list)
Certiorari granted 27 April 2026, limited to the following questions (as the Court's order states them):
1. Whether Article III of the Constitution precludes the Department of Labor from adjudicating proceedings to collect monetary remedies from employers who have allegedly violated the terms and conditions of employment of H-2A workers and domestic workers in corresponding employment.
2. Whether 8 U. S. C. §1188(g)(2) authorizes the Department of Labor to adjudicate proceedings to collect monetary remedies from employers who have allegedly violated the terms and conditions of employment of H-2A workers and domestic workers in corresponding employment.

(The petition's question, which the Court reformulated and supplemented: "The H-2A visa program grants U.S. employers the privilege of 'import[ing]' foreign aliens into the United States for temporary agricultural work under special visas. 8 U.S.C. 1188(a)(1). As a condition of granting petitions to import foreign workers, federal law requires an employer to agree to comply with statutory and regulatory requirements governing workers' living and working conditions. Congress has authorized the Secretary of Labor to impose monetary remedies to assure employer compliance with those 'terms and conditions.' 8 U.S.C. 1188(g)(2). The question presented is: Whether Article III of the Constitution precludes Congress from assigning to the Secretary of Labor the initial adjudication of proceedings to collect monetary remedies from employers who violate the terms and conditions of participating in the H-2A visa program.")

## Lower court and holding (two sentences)
United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, 148 F.4th 121 (No. 23-2608; Hardiman, J., for a panel with Porter and Smith, JJ.). Held: the Department of Labor's proceeding to recover back wages and civil money penalties from an H-2A employer for violations of the job order's terms is in substance a common-law contract action seeking legal remedies and therefore concerns private rights that must be adjudicated by an Article III court under SEC v. Jarkesy; the public-rights exception for immigration does not reach labour-certification rules on wages, housing, meals and transportation, so the district court's dismissal of the employer's challenge was reversed (the statutory question was not reached).

## Procedural posture
Respondent Sun Valley Orchards, a New Jersey fruit and vegetable farm, employed 96 H-2A workers and 51 domestic workers in 2015 to pick asparagus and peppers. The Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division found violations of the H-2A terms and conditions — undisclosed meal charges and drink deductions, housing conditions, unsafe and unlicensed transportation, and the three-fourths work guarantee — and an administrative law judge (28 October 2019), affirmed by the Administrative Review Board (27 May 2021), assessed $211,800 in civil money penalties and $344,945.80 in back wages. Sun Valley sued in the District of New Jersey, which dismissed, holding the matter within the public-rights doctrine. The Third Circuit reversed on Article III grounds and directed judgment for the company. Certiorari granted 27 April 2026, limited to the two questions above; the second (statutory authority under §1188(g)(2)) was argued below but not decided there. Respondent is represented by the Institute for Justice. Set for argument 10 November 2026. (The Department of Labor is the petitioner and lost below; a vote for petitioner is a vote to reverse, a vote for respondent a vote to affirm.)

## Petitioner's core arguments (three bullets, from the merits brief)
- Article III reserves private rights to the courts, but under Jarkesy's "historic categories" approach immigration is a settled area of public rights: the sovereign's power to control the entry of aliens (Nishimura Ekiu; Fong Yue Ting; Lem Moon Sing; Department of State v. Muñoz) has always included the power to enforce the conditions of entry through executive-imposed money penalties, as Oceanic Steam Navigation Co. v. Stranahan and Lloyd Sabaudo Società v. Elting hold, and Murray's Lessee recognises the Executive's authority over the public revenue.
- An H-2A petition is a government-conferred privilege to import foreign workers on conditions the government sets, and a recipient of such a privilege may be required to accept non-Article III adjudication of matters tied to it (Oil States; McElrath; Allman; Thomas v. Union Carbide); the Third Circuit's contract analogy fails because the proceeding enforces a public right to compliance with government-imposed, non-waivable conditions (29 C.F.R. §501.5), not a bargained-for contract, overlap with common-law claims does not matter (Allman; Oceanic Steam; Tull), civil penalties are permissible in public-rights cases (Jarkesy n.2), and back wages are equitable relief that restores compliance (Albemarle Paper Co. v. Moody).
- Section 1188(g)(2) authorises the Department's adjudication: the Secretary may "impos[e] appropriate penalties" — a word that, by contrast with "seek", denotes agency rather than court proceedings (as Jarkesy's own usage reflects) — and may "take such actions" as are necessary to assure compliance, which covers back wages (NLRB v. J.H. Rutter-Rex; Albemarle); the Department's 1987 regulations, issued months after enactment, read the statute that way from the start, and 28 U.S.C. §2461 does not apply where the statute specifies the mode of recovery; the judgment should be reversed.

## Respondent's core arguments (three bullets, from the merits brief)
- The Department's claims are private, contract-based claims for common-law remedies: the job order functions as the workers' employment contract, civil penalties (Tull v. United States) and back pay (Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers Local 391 v. Terry) are legal relief, Granfinanciera and Stern v. Marshall confirm that such claims belong in Article III courts with a jury, and under Jarkesy the matter is therefore presumptively one of private right; Murray's Lessee's sovereign-revenue tradition does not help the Department.
- The immigration exception is narrow: Oceanic Steam and Lloyd Sabaudo concerned penalties on carriers tied to admission and collected as a condition of clearance at the border, whereas penalties on domestic employers of foreign workers were historically adjudicated in court (Hepner v. United States; the contract-labor law of 1885), and the violations here concern kitchen access, housing and transport for a workforce half of whom were U.S. citizens; the power to grant or revoke a privilege does not carry the power to adjudicate monetary liability (Oil States; Markman), consent cannot be implied from regulated participation (College Savings Bank), and if Atlas Roofing stands in the way it should be overruled.
- Section 1188(g)(2) does not clearly authorise in-house adjudication: "impose" does not mean "adjudicate", the "necessary actions" clause says nothing about where liability is determined, and a clear statement is required before an agency may adjudicate (Bank One; Sherman & Sons); the judgment should be affirmed on Article III grounds, or alternatively on the statutory ground.
